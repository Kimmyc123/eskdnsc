"End Stage Chronic Kidney Disease” Annotated Bibliography

Gupta R, Woo K, Yi AJ. Epidemiology of end-stage kidney disease. Semin Vasc Surg. 2021;34(1):71-78. doi:10.1053/j.semvascsurg.2021.02.010.

This comprehensive review examines the epidemiology of end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) with an emphasis on trends in incidence and prevalence, causes, and treatment modalities in the United States. Gupta and colleagues synthesize data from national registries and the published literature, highlighting that diabetes, hypertension, and glomerular disease remain the principal etiologies of ESKD. The review details that ESKD incidence and prevalence have steadily risen, with significant racial and ethnic disparities, particularly affecting Blacks, Hispanics, and older adults. In terms of clinical presentation, ESKD is characterized by a severely reduced glomerular filtration rate (GFR less than 15 mL/min/1.73 m²) or other markers of kidney damage, with common symptoms including fatigue, edema, and signs of volume overload. Treatment options are comprehensively discussed; while dialysis (both hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis) remains the mainstay, kidney transplantation is underscored as providing superior longterm survival benefits. The article also notes statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) in outcome measures among treatment modalities and highlights the additional burden imposed by the COVID-19 pandemic, marked by higher hospitalization and mortality rates among ESKD patients. Key strengths of this review include its broad scope, integration of cost and patient-reported outcomes, and focus on vascular access issues and disparities, while limitations primarily relate to its review design and focus on US data, which may impact extrapolation to other regions. This resource offers valuable insights by linking epidemiological trends to clinical presentation, treatment outcomes, and healthcare policy implications.

Thurlow J, Joshi M, Yan G., et al. Global epidemiology of end-stage kidney disease and disparities in kidney replacement therapy. Am J Nephrol. 2021;52(2):98-107. doi:10.11590/0000514550

This article presents a comprehensive review of the global epidemiology of end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) and disparities in kidney replacement therapy (KRT) access worldwide. The authors conducted an extensive analysis of international registry data and published literature to examine variations in ESKD incidence, prevalence, and treatment outcomes across different regions. The findings reveal that while incidence rates of treated ESKD have stabilized in many high-income countries, they have increased substantially in East and Southeast Asia. Statistical analysis demonstrates significant disparities in KRT access between high-income and low-to-middle-income countries, with unadjusted 5-year survival rates showing marked differences: 41% in the United States, 48% in Europe, and 60% in Japan (p less than 0.001). The study highlights that the number of people dying prematurely due to lack of KRT access is estimated to be up to three times higher than those receiving treatment in resource-limited settings. The authors identify variations in dialysis practices, including vascular access methods, session duration, and treatment adequacy as significant contributors to differences in patient outcomes globally. A key strength of this research is its global perspective, providing valuable comparative data on ESKD management across diverse healthcare systems. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in data consistency across countries, which may affect the reliability of some comparisons. This article serves as an essential reference for understanding the international landscape of ESKD management and provides critical evidence to inform healthcare policy and future research initiatives addressing global kidney disease burden.

Zha Y, Qian Q. Protein nutrition and malnutrition in CKD and ESRD. Nutrients. 2017;9(3):208. doi:10.3390/nu9030208

This article examines the complex interplay between protein nutrition and malnutrition in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) and end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Zha and Qian review how metabolic acidosis, systemic inflammation, hormonal dysregulation, and the accumulation of uremic toxins contribute to hypercatabolism and a negative nitrogen balance in CKD/ESRD. They provide practical nutritional recommendations: for non-dialysis CKD patients, an intake of 0.6–0.8 g/kg/day (with >50% high biological value proteins) is advised, whereas for patients on peritoneal or hemodialysis, 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day is recommended, with a daily energy intake of 30–35 kcal/kg based on ideal body weight. Although the article does not present any explicit statistical data or p values, it establishes that adequate protein intake is critical for reducing the risk of protein-energy wasting. A notable strength of this work is its comprehensive review of the derangements leading to protein malnutrition; however, the lack of original research data and reliance on retrospective analyses and secondary evidence are acknowledged limitations. This resource is valuable for explaining the rationale behind nutritional strategies in CKD and ESRD and for discussing approaches to prevent malnutrition while optimizing dialysis outcomes.

Okamura M, Inoue T, Ogawa M, et al. Rehabilitation nutrition in patients with chronic kidney disease and cachexia. Nutrients. 2022;14:4722. doi:10.3390/nu14224722

This review article summarizes current evidence regarding rehabilitation nutrition interventions for patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) and cachexia. It emphasizes that as CKD progresses to end stage kidney disease (ESKD), there is a critical shift in dietary needs requiring more aggressive nutritional and exercise interventions to counter protein-energy wasting and loss of skeletal muscle mass. The article advocates a multidimensional approach combining nutritional therapy and physical rehabilitation, including personalized dietary planning and goal setting, to improve patient outcomes. This evidence provides a scientific basis for discussing nutrition therapy in the podcast, particularly the dietary modifications necessary during the transition from CKD to ESKD. Cupisti A, Kovesdy CP, D'Alessandro C, et al. Dietary approach to recurrent or chronic hyperkalemia in patients with decreased kidney function. Nutrients. 2018;10(3):261. doi:10.3390/nu10030261

This review article examines dietary interventions for managing recurrent or chronic hyperkalemia in patients with decreased kidney function, particularly those with chronic kidney disease (CKD) and end-stage kidney disease (ESKD). The authors provide a comprehensive overview of dietary strategies to control potassium levels, including limiting potassium intake to less than 3 g per day, increasing fiber consumption to promote beneficial gut bacteria growth, and reducing acid load through appropriate fruit and vegetable selection. The article emphasizes the importance of patient education regarding high-potassium foods, cooking methods that reduce potassium content, and awareness of hidden potassium sources such as food additives and salt substitutes. While the review does not present original statistical data or significance values, it synthesizes current clinical guidelines and evidence-based recommendations for hyperkalemia management. A notable strength of this work is its practical approach to integrating dietary modifications with pharmacological interventions in a comprehensive treatment strategy. However, as a narrative review rather than an experimental study, it does not contribute new clinical data to the field. This resource provides valuable insights for healthcare professionals developing nutritional plans for CKD and ESKD patients at risk of hyperkalemia, offering a balanced perspective on dietary management within the broader context of kidney disease care. This study will be beneficial for explaining how nutrition plays a role in managing potassium levels in dialysis patients.

AbiKharma J, ElNakouzi J, Abboud M, et al. Malnutrition and its risk factors in renal patients on dialysis. Proc Nutr Soc. 2024;83(OCE4):E428. doi:10.1017/S0029665124006669

This study assessed malnutrition prevalence and associated risk factors among patients with end stage kidney disease (ESKD) on hemodialysis in Lebanon. Using a cross sectional design, the authors evaluated 119 patients from two dialysis centers by collecting demographic data, nutritional intake via 24-hour recall and food frequency questionnaires, anthropometric measures, biochemical markers, and body composition (via bioelectrical impedance measured post‐dialysis). They also assessed nutritional knowledge and treatment adherence using validated questionnaires. A striking 85.5% of participants met the Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition (GLIM) criteria for malnutrition, with lower education, poor nutrition knowledge, and inadequate caloric intake being prominent among those affected. This study underscores the necessity for individualized nutrition strategies to improve compliance and mitigate malnutrition risk in ESKD patients, supporting my discussion on personalized dietary interventions in dialysis care.
